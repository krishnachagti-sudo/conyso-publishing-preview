# Did William of Ockham write "Entities should not be multiplied beyond necessity"?

Just show me the answer

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** The famous Latin sentence is nowhere in Ockham's surviving works. Its earliest close version was written by John Punch in 1639, nearly three centuries after Ockham died. Ockham argued for the same principle, in other words.

Tell someone: The famous Latin line of Occam's razor is not in Ockham's works; John Punch wrote a close version in 1639, nearly three centuries after Ockham died.

## What's the version people tell?

The popular version is that the medieval friar William of Ockham wrote *Entia non sunt multiplicanda praeter necessitatem*, "entities should not be multiplied beyond necessity," and so gave his name to the razor.
