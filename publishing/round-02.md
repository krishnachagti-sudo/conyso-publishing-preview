### [Did William of Ockham write "Entities should not be multiplied beyond necessity"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-william-of-ockham-write-entities-should-not-be-multiplied-beyond-necessity.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** The famous Latin sentence is nowhere in Ockham's surviving works. Its earliest close version was written by John Punch in 1639, nearly three centuries after Ockham died. Ockham argued for the same principle, in other words.

### [Did Hebb say "neurons that fire together wire together"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-hebb-say-neurons-that-fire-together-wire-together.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Donald Hebb set out the rule in 1949. The slogan is not his. It came in 1992, usually credited to Carla Shatz as a mnemonic, and crediting it to Hebb is, in the record's words, the most common error about the rule.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-hebb-say-neurons-that-fire-together-wire-together.html)

Round 2 · No. 13

### [Did Mark Twain say "If all you have is a hammer, everything looks like a nail"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-mark-twain-say-if-all-you-have-is-a-hammer-everything-looks-like-a-nail.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** There is no evidence that Mark Twain said it. The idea was named by the philosopher Abraham Kaplan in 1964, after a talk in 1962. Its best-known hammer-and-nail form is Abraham Maslow's, from 1966.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-mark-twain-say-if-all-you-have-is-a-hammer-everything-looks-like-a-nail.html)

**Folklore.** The famous sentence is Marilyn Strathern's, from 1997. Charles Goodhart's original point, in 1975, was narrower and about monetary policy.

### [Did a real Robert Hanlon coin Hanlon's razor?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-a-real-robert-hanlon-coin-hanlons-razor.html)

DISPUTED

**Disputed.** The razor is generally credited to a Robert J. Hanlon, but the evidence for him is second-hand. Its first appearance in print, in a 1980 book, gives the name "Hanlon" with no indication of who he was.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-a-real-robert-hanlon-coin-hanlons-razor.html)

### [Did Napoleon say "Never attribute to malice what can be explained by stupidity"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-napoleon-say-never-attribute-to-malice-what-can-be-explained-by-stupidity.html)

**Folklore.** The line is not Napoleon's. Quote Investigator, the source the record cites for its dated history of the saying, debunks the Napoleon attribution. The earliest known printing of the razor is from 1980.

### [Did Godwin say whoever mentions Hitler first loses?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-godwin-say-whoever-mentions-hitler-first-loses.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Mike Godwin's 1990 law says only that a long online argument is likely to produce a comparison to Hitler or the Nazis. The rule that whoever makes the comparison loses was added later by the Usenet community, not by Godwin.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-godwin-say-whoever-mentions-hitler-first-loses.html)

Round 2 · No. 18

### [Did Sturgeon say "Ninety percent of everything is crap"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-sturgeon-say-ninety-percent-of-everything-is-crap.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Theodore Sturgeon's printed word was "crud," not "crap." His *Venture Science Fiction* column first printed it in September 1957. In March 1958 the column gave it as "Ninety percent of everything is crud." He also called it his Revelation, not his law.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-sturgeon-say-ninety-percent-of-everything-is-crap.html)

Round 2 · No. 19

### [Did Twain or Einstein say "A man with one watch knows what time it is"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-twain-or-einstein-say-a-man-with-one-watch-knows-what-time-it-is.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Neither did. The saying first appears without a name in the *San Diego Union* in 1930. The attributions to Mark Twain and Albert Einstein are unsupported, and no documented Segal wrote it either.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-twain-or-einstein-say-a-man-with-one-watch-knows-what-time-it-is.html)

Round 2 · No. 20

### [Did Henry Kissinger say academic politics are vicious because the stakes are so small?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-henry-kissinger-say-academic-politics-are-vicious-because-the-stakes-are-so-small.html)

DISPUTED

**Disputed.** The line has been credited to Henry Kissinger, but he is one of several claimants, and none has a clean title. The record calls the attribution famously tangled. Its usual namesake is the Columbia political scientist Wallace Sayre, and Quote Investigator traces the idea back to Samuel Johnson in 1765.
