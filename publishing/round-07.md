### [Does "red herring" come from training hounds with smoked fish?](https://conyso.com/publishing/does-red-herring-come-from-training-hounds-with-smoked-fish.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** The hound-training origin is likely a myth. The figurative sense of "red herring" is real, and it is traceable to the journalist William Cobbett in 1807.

Round 7 · No. 62

### [Is the "invisible hand" the heart of Adam Smith's economics?](https://conyso.com/publishing/is-the-invisible-hand-the-heart-of-adam-smiths-economics.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Adam Smith used the exact phrase "invisible hand" only three times in his works, and just once in *The Wealth of Nations*. It was not the center of his economics, and he supported many roles for government.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/is-the-invisible-hand-the-heart-of-adam-smiths-economics.html)

Round 7 · No. 63

### [Did Say write "supply creates its own demand"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-say-write-supply-creates-its-own-demand.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** The phrase "supply creates its own demand" does not appear in Jean-Baptiste Say's own writing. John Maynard Keynes popularized it in 1936, and even Keynes seems to have been quoting a phrase already current.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-say-write-supply-creates-its-own-demand.html)

Round 7 · No. 64

### [Did habeas corpus come from Magna Carta?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-habeas-corpus-come-from-magna-carta.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Habeas corpus did not come from Magna Carta. Its roots predate the charter of 1215. It grew out of English common law over centuries, and the Habeas Corpus Act of 1679 codified it.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-habeas-corpus-come-from-magna-carta.html)

Round 7 · No. 65

### [Did Rousseau coin "the noble savage"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-rousseau-coin-the-noble-savage.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Jean-Jacques Rousseau did not coin "the noble savage." The phrase does not appear in his writings and was pinned on him later.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-rousseau-coin-the-noble-savage.html)

Round 7 · No. 66

### [Did Clausewitz coin "the fog of war"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-clausewitz-coin-the-fog-of-war.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Carl von Clausewitz did not coin "the fog of war." In *On War* (1832) he conveyed the uncertainty of war through images of fog, twilight and moonlight. The crisp phrase is a later paraphrase, prominent partly because a twentieth-century translation chose the word "fog."

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-clausewitz-coin-the-fog-of-war.html)

Round 7 · No. 67

### [Was the acronym "MAD" a critic's jibe?](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-the-acronym-mad-a-critics-jibe.html)

TRUE

**True.** The acronym MAD is attributed to a critic, Donald Brennan of the Hudson Institute, who used it derisively to argue that the nuclear posture it named was irrational. Robert McNamara, under whom the doctrine took shape, promoted "assured destruction," not MAD.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-the-acronym-mad-a-critics-jibe.html)

Round 7 · No. 68

### [Was "moving the goalposts" used in Parliament a century ago?](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-moving-the-goalposts-used-in-parliament-a-century-ago.html)

TRUE

**True.** The earliest documented figurative use of the phrase is in a House of Commons debate on 2 May 1924. The idiom is British, and it was in political speech some sixty years before it came into widespread use in the 1980s.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-moving-the-goalposts-used-in-parliament-a-century-ago.html)

Round 7 · No. 69

### [Did Weber write "iron cage"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-weber-write-iron-cage.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Max Weber did not write "iron cage." The English phrase is Talcott Parsons's 1930 translation of Weber's German *stahlhartes Gehäuse*, which more literally means a shell or casing as hard as steel.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-weber-write-iron-cage.html)

Round 7 · No. 70

### [Did Richard Dawkins coin the word "meme"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-richard-dawkins-coin-the-word-meme.html)

TRUE

**True.** Richard Dawkins coined the word "meme" in his 1976 book *The Selfish Gene*, as the name for a unit of cultural transmission.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-richard-dawkins-coin-the-word-meme.html)
