# About Conyso Publishing

A small imprint with one rule: a book should check the story before it tells it.

## Why does it exist?

Conyso built the Law Tome and the Bias Atlas as free, sourced references: what each named law or bias says, where it came from, and how well it holds up. Working on them turned up the same thing again and again. Many of the famous stories attached to these ideas are wrong, misattributed or still argued over, and the correct versions are often better stories.

Conyso Publishing exists to put those findings into books people actually pick up: a quiz for the dinner table, a reference for the shelf. The datasets stay free online. The books are the edited, designed, offline version.

## What does it publish?

Quiz and reference books in two series, one for each dataset. The first title is [*True or Folklore?*](https://conyso.com/publishing/true-or-folklore.html), 100 famous stories put to the test, coming soon in paperback and Kindle. [The series page](https://conyso.com/publishing/series.html) explains how a dataset becomes a series.

## How is it connected to Conyso?

Conyso Publishing is Conyso's imprint, the name on the spine of its books. Conyso publishes the two datasets the books are built from, and the books link back to them: every answer names its source and points to the record it comes from. [Conyso's home page](https://conyso.com/index.html) has the rest of the company.

## Who is the editor?

Krishna Chagti founded Conyso, which publishes the Law Tome and the Bias Atlas, and he edits the books built from them. He chooses what goes into each book, reads and approves the finished book before it goes on sale, and is the name on its cover: the books are credited to him as editor, not to an invented author.

His other work, from Conyso's ventures to his research papers, is on [his founder site](https://conyso.com/founder/).

## How do you report a mistake?

Every book prints the address of its [corrections page](https://conyso.com/lawtome/errata/) in the front matter. A mistake found after print goes there, with what changed. [How corrections work](https://conyso.com/publishing/standards.html#corrections) is in our standards.
