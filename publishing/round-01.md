Round 1 · No. 1

### [Did Archimedes shout "Eureka!" in the bath?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-archimedes-shout-eureka-in-the-bath.html)

**Folklore.** Archimedes stated the law of buoyancy around 250 BC, in his treatise *On Floating Bodies*. The bath, the crown and the cry of "Eureka!" are almost certainly a later embellishment.

FOLKLORE

Round 1 · No. 2

### [Did an apple fall on Newton's head?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-an-apple-fall-on-newtons-head.html)

**Folklore.** There is an apple in Newton's own account of his thinking about gravity. It does not hit him on the head. That detail is a later embellishment.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-an-apple-fall-on-newtons-head.html)

![Cartoon for the question: Did Newton himself tell the apple story?](https://conyso.com/publishing/art/003.png)

TRUE

Round 1 · No. 3

### [Did Newton himself tell the apple story?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-newton-himself-tell-the-apple-story.html)

**True.** The apple story is not a fable invented by Newton's admirers. According to *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, it traces back to Newton's own later recollection. Only the apple hitting his head was added afterward.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-newton-himself-tell-the-apple-story.html)

![Cartoon for the question: Did Galileo drop balls from the Leaning Tower of Pisa?](https://conyso.com/publishing/art/004.png)

FOLKLORE

Round 1 · No. 4

### [Did Galileo drop balls from the Leaning Tower of Pisa?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-galileo-drop-balls-from-the-leaning-tower-of-pisa.html)

**Folklore.** The tower drop is likely a legend. It is not in Galileo's own writings. His documented work on falling bodies used balls rolling down ramps, with notes from around 1603 to 1604 and results published in 1638.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-galileo-drop-balls-from-the-leaning-tower-of-pisa.html)

![Cartoon for the question: Did Pythagoras discover the Pythagorean theorem?](https://conyso.com/publishing/art/005.png)

FOLKLORE

Round 1 · No. 5

### [Did Pythagoras discover the Pythagorean theorem?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-pythagoras-discover-the-pythagorean-theorem.html)

**Folklore.** The relation that carries Pythagoras's name was known and used long before him. A Babylonian tablet from around 1800 BC lists its number triples, and Pythagoras lived from about 570 to 495 BC.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-pythagoras-discover-the-pythagorean-theorem.html)

![Cartoon for the question: Did Einstein write E = mc² in his 1905 paper?](https://conyso.com/publishing/art/006.png)

FOLKLORE

Round 1 · No. 6

### [Did Einstein write E = mc² in his 1905 paper?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-einstein-write-e-mc2-in-his-1905-paper.html)

**Folklore.** Einstein's 1905 paper contains the idea but not the famous formula. It states in a single sentence that a body which gives off energy as radiation loses mass in proportion.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-einstein-write-e-mc2-in-his-1905-paper.html)

![Cartoon for the question: Did Einstein win his Nobel Prize for something other than relativity?](https://conyso.com/publishing/art/007.png)

TRUE

Round 1 · No. 7

### [Did Einstein win his Nobel Prize for something other than relativity?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-einstein-win-his-nobel-prize-for-something-other-than-relativity.html)

**True.** Einstein's Nobel Prize, the physics prize designated for 1921, was for his work on the photoelectric effect. NobelPrize.org gives the citation as "for his services to Theoretical Physics, and especially for his discovery of the law of the photoelectric effect." Relativity is not what the prize named.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-einstein-win-his-nobel-prize-for-something-other-than-relativity.html)

![Cartoon for the question: Did Gordon Moore say chips double every 18 months?](https://conyso.com/publishing/art/008.png)

FOLKLORE

Round 1 · No. 8

### [Did Gordon Moore say chips double every 18 months?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-gordon-moore-say-chips-double-every-18-months.html)

**Folklore.** Gordon Moore never gave 18 months. His 1965 article predicted that the number of components on a chip would double roughly every year, and in 1975 he revised that to every two years.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-gordon-moore-say-chips-double-every-18-months.html)

![Cartoon for the question: Did Churchill promise "blood, sweat and tears"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/art/009.png)

FOLKLORE

Round 1 · No. 9

### [Did Churchill promise "blood, sweat and tears"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-churchill-promise-blood-sweat-and-tears.html)

**Folklore.** In May 1940 Churchill offered "blood, toil, tears and sweat." That is four items, not three, and "toil" is the word that dropped out.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-churchill-promise-blood-sweat-and-tears.html)

![Cartoon for the question: Did Edward Murphy say "Anything that can go wrong will go wrong"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/art/010.png)

DISPUTED

Round 1 · No. 10

### [Did Edward Murphy say "Anything that can go wrong will go wrong"?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-edward-murphy-say-anything-that-can-go-wrong-will-go-wrong.html)

**Disputed.** A maxim like it came out of an Air Force rocket-sled project around 1949, and Murphy's name stuck to it. Who first said it, and in what words, is not settled. The earliest firm printing under Murphy's name, from 1952, credits an unnamed physicist.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-edward-murphy-say-anything-that-can-go-wrong-will-go-wrong.html)
