### [Was Photo 51 taken by Raymond Gosling?](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-photo-51-taken-by-raymond-gosling.html)

TRUE

**True.** Raymond Gosling, a doctoral student, took Photo 51 at King's College London in May 1952, while working under Rosalind Franklin's supervision. The credit belongs to both of them.

Round 9 · No. 82

### [Did Watson and Crick steal Rosalind Franklin's data?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-watson-and-crick-steal-rosalind-franklins-data.html)

DISPUTED

**Disputed.** The record documents that Maurice Wilkins showed Photo 51 to James Watson without Franklin's knowledge, and that her data shaped the 1953 model. Recent scholarship argues that this was not a simple story of theft.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-watson-and-crick-steal-rosalind-franklins-data.html)

Round 9 · No. 83

### [Did Philippa Foot invent the trolley problem?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-philippa-foot-invent-the-trolley-problem.html)

TRUE

**True.** Philippa Foot introduced the original case in 1967, in a paper called "The Problem of Abortion and the Doctrine of the Double Effect." She did not name it. Judith Jarvis Thomson called it the trolley problem and added its best-known variants in 1976 and 1985.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-philippa-foot-invent-the-trolley-problem.html)

Round 9 · No. 84

### [Was Wegener the first to notice the continents fit together?](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-wegener-the-first-to-notice-the-continents-fit-together.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Alfred Wegener was not the first. Ortelius and others remarked on the jigsaw fit of the continents centuries before him. Wegener's originality, first put to geologists in a 1912 lecture, lay in combining that fit with fossil, rock and climate evidence.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-wegener-the-first-to-notice-the-continents-fit-together.html)

Round 9 · No. 85

### [Was umami discovered in a seaweed broth?](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-umami-discovered-in-a-seaweed-broth.html)

TRUE

**True.** In 1908 the Japanese chemist Kikunae Ikeda isolated glutamate from kombu seaweed broth, coined the word umami and developed MSG as a seasoning. Umami is now widely accepted as the fifth basic taste.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-umami-discovered-in-a-seaweed-broth.html)

Round 9 · No. 86

### [Did Mendeleev invent the periodic table alone?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-mendeleev-invent-the-periodic-table-alone.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Dmitri Mendeleev published his periodic table in 1869, but he did not conceive it alone. John Newlands had proposed a related law of octaves in 1865, and Julius Lothar Meyer worked out a similar arrangement independently at about the same time.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-mendeleev-invent-the-periodic-table-alone.html)

Round 9 · No. 87

### [Were Mendel's pea results too good to be true?](https://conyso.com/publishing/were-mendels-pea-results-too-good-to-be-true.html)

DISPUTED

**Disputed.** In 1936 Fisher began a statistical debate over whether Gregor Mendel's reported ratios fit too closely to be chance. The record calls the question unresolved, not a proven charge of fabrication.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/were-mendels-pea-results-too-good-to-be-true.html)

Round 9 · No. 88

### [Did Gauss guess the prime number theorem as a teenager?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-gauss-guess-the-prime-number-theorem-as-a-teenager.html)

TRUE

**True.** The record reports that Gauss, as a teenager in the 1790s, conjectured the prime number theorem from tables of primes. It was proved only in 1896, about a century later.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-gauss-guess-the-prime-number-theorem-as-a-teenager.html)

Round 9 · No. 89

### [Did Hooke publish his law as an anagram?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-hooke-publish-his-law-as-an-anagram.html)

TRUE

**True.** Robert Hooke published his law of springs in 1676 as the anagram "ceiiinosssttuu." In 1678 he revealed the answer: "ut tensio, sic vis," as the extension, so the force.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-hooke-publish-his-law-as-an-anagram.html)

Round 9 · No. 90

### [Was the 1919 eclipse a clean proof of Einstein?](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-the-1919-eclipse-a-clean-proof-of-einstein.html)

DISPUTED

**Disputed.** The 1919 eclipse measurements matched Einstein's prediction, and modern reanalyses largely uphold the result. But the proof was not clean: blurred plates from one telescope were discarded, and historians have debated whether that was justified.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-the-1919-eclipse-a-clean-proof-of-einstein.html)
