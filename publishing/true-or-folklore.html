True or Folklore?

100 Famous Stories You've Been Told Wrong: The Myth-Busting Quiz for Curious Minds

Edited by Krishna Chagti · Conyso Publishing · Paperback and Kindle planned

Play round 1 free See the ten rounds

Did Newton get hit by an apple? Did Archimedes really shout "Eureka!" in the bath? Did Galileo drop balls from the Leaning Tower?

Everyone has heard these stories. Fewer people know which ones actually happened.

True or Folklore? puts 100 famous stories from science, history, money and the mind to the test. Each one starts as a question on its own page, with a cartoon and three boxes to tick: TRUE, FOLKLORE or DISPUTED. Then turn the page for the verdict, the real story, why the myth spread, and the earliest source that settles it.

The gift

  • A page at the front to write who it's for
  • Score titles from "Easily Fooled" to "Myth Buster" to argue over
  • A "Tell someone" fact at the end of every answer, for the next dinner conversation

What they'll keep using

  • 100 stories in 10 ready-made quiz rounds for game nights, with a two-team scoresheet
  • Two minutes per story, so it works for a commute or a coffee break
  • Every verdict checked against the record, with the source named on the page. Eighteen stories are still genuinely disputed, and the book says so
  • A page of ideas for using it in class

What does the book have that this site doesn't?

  • The full answers. The site gives each verdict and the fact worth repeating. The book adds the real story, why the myth spread and the earliest source, about two minutes a story.

Where do the stories come from?

The Law Tome series is built from the Law Tome, a free, sourced directory of more than a thousand named laws, effects and principles. Each answer links back to the law it comes from. The text is drafted with AI from sourced records and checked against those sources. The illustrations are made with AI. Corrections found after print are listed on the errata page, and the Law Tome's books page lists every title built from it.

Questions about the book

Who is True or Folklore? for?

Anyone who likes being the person who knows the real version: quiz hosts, family tables, classrooms and curious readers. It is written as a gift, with a page at the front to write who it is for.

How long does each story take?

About two minutes: the question on one page, the answer on the next. That makes it work for a commute, a coffee break or a round at a party.

How many of the stories are true?

34 of the 100. 48 are folklore, and 18 are still genuinely disputed, which the book says rather than pretending to settle.

Where do the verdicts come from?

From the Law Tome's sourced records, checked again against a primary source, or the best available, for the book. The source is named on the page.

What age is it for?

It is written for adults and teens. A few stories deal with a murder, war and psychology experiments that harmed people, so read a round before using it with younger students.

What formats will it come in?

Paperback and Kindle are planned. There is no store link yet because it is not on sale yet.
