Story No. 15 · Round 2: Who really said it?

Did a real Robert Hanlon coin Hanlon's razor?

Your call: true, folklore or disputed?

How sure?

DISPUTED

Disputed. The razor is generally credited to a Robert J. Hanlon, but the evidence for him is second-hand. Its first appearance in print, in a 1980 book, gives the name "Hanlon" with no indication of who he was.

Tell someone: Hanlon's razor first appeared in print in a 1980 book, but the evidence that a real Robert J. Hanlon wrote it is second-hand.

What's the version people tell?

The popular version is that a man named Hanlon coined the line "Never attribute to malice that which is adequately explained by stupidity," and that the razor bears his name for that reason.
