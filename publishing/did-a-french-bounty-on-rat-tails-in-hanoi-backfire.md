# Did a French bounty on rat tails in Hanoi backfire?

Just show me the answer

TRUE

**True.** In 1902 the French colonial administration in Hanoi paid a bounty for each rat killed, with a severed tail as proof. Rat-catchers began cutting the tails off live rats and letting them go to breed. The case is documented in a 2003 paper by M. G. Vann in *French Colonial History*.

Tell someone: Hanoi's 1902 rat bounty took tails as proof, so rat-catchers cut the tails off live rats and released them to breed.

## What's the version people tell?

The popular version is the same story, which is the surprise. Paying for tails produced rats without tails, and rats without tails went on producing rats. It sounds too neat to be true, and it shares a shape with the Delhi cobra story, which the record treats as folklore.
