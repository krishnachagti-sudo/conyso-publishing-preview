Was "moving the goalposts" used in Parliament a century ago?

Your call: true, folklore or disputed?

How sure?

TRUE

True. The earliest documented figurative use of the phrase is in a House of Commons debate on 2 May 1924. The idiom is British, and it was in political speech some sixty years before it came into widespread use in the 1980s.

Tell someone: In the House of Commons on 2 May 1924, the Labour MP George Hardie said the Liberals "wanted to shift the goal posts."

What's the version people tell?

The popular assumption dates the phrase to the 1980s and files it with that decade's business jargon.
