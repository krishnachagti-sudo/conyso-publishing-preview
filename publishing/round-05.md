### [Did Stigler credit Stigler's law to someone else?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-stigler-credit-stiglers-law-to-someone-else.html)

TRUE

**True.** In the 1980 paper that named the law after himself, the statistician Stephen Stigler credited its discovery to the sociologist Robert K. Merton. The law says no scientific discovery is named after its original discoverer, so it was built to obey itself.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-stigler-credit-stiglers-law-to-someone-else.html)

Round 5 · No. 42

### [Did Maxwell write the four Maxwell's equations?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-maxwell-write-the-four-maxwells-equations.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** James Clerk Maxwell built the theory, but the four compact equations that carry his name are Oliver Heaviside's reformulation of 1884. Maxwell's own 1865 paper contained twenty equations.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-maxwell-write-the-four-maxwells-equations.html)

Round 5 · No. 44

### [Did Thomas Gresham formulate Gresham's law?](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-thomas-gresham-formulate-greshams-law.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Thomas Gresham did not formulate Gresham's law. Henry Dunning Macleod named it after him in 1858, and the principle had been stated long before: by Oresme, by Copernicus around 1519, and in Aristophanes' play *The Frogs* around 405 BC.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/did-thomas-gresham-formulate-greshams-law.html)

Round 5 · No. 45

### [Was Hubble the first to find Hubble's law?](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-hubble-the-first-to-find-hubbles-law.html)

FOLKLORE

**Folklore.** Georges Lemaître derived the relation between a galaxy's distance and its speed of recession, and estimated its constant, in 1927. Edwin Hubble's paper came two years later, in 1929. In 2018 the International Astronomical Union recommended renaming the law the Hubble–Lemaître law.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-hubble-the-first-to-find-hubbles-law.html)

Round 5 · No. 47

### [Does Laffer himself say the Laffer curve wasn't his idea?](https://conyso.com/publishing/does-laffer-himself-say-the-laffer-curve-wasnt-his-idea.html)

TRUE

**True.** Arthur Laffer has disclaimed originality for the curve that bears his name. He credits earlier sources, including Ibn Khaldun in the fourteenth century and John Maynard Keynes.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/does-laffer-himself-say-the-laffer-curve-wasnt-his-idea.html)

Round 5 · No. 48

### [Was the Overton window named only after Overton's death?](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-the-overton-window-named-only-after-overtons-death.html)

TRUE

**True.** Joseph P. Overton developed the idea in the mid-1990s, but it was his colleague Joseph Lehman who named and elaborated it, after Overton died in a 2003 ultralight-aircraft crash.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/was-the-overton-window-named-only-after-overtons-death.html)

Round 5 · No. 49

### [Is the Baader–Meinhof phenomenon named after a militant group?](https://conyso.com/publishing/is-the-baader-meinhof-phenomenon-named-after-a-militant-group.html)

TRUE

**True.** The nickname comes from the West German militant group. It traces to a 1994 reader submission to the *St. Paul Pioneer Press* from a writer who had heard the group's name twice in quick succession.

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/is-the-baader-meinhof-phenomenon-named-after-a-militant-group.html)

Round 5 · No. 50

### [Is "Muphry's law" misspelled on purpose?](https://conyso.com/publishing/is-muphrys-law-misspelled-on-purpose.html)

TRUE

**True.** The misspelling is deliberate. John Bangsund, of the Society of Editors (Victoria) in Australia, coined Muphry's law in the society's March 1992 newsletter as "the editorial application of the better-known Murphy's law."

[The answer and its source →](https://conyso.com/publishing/is-muphrys-law-misspelled-on-purpose.html)
