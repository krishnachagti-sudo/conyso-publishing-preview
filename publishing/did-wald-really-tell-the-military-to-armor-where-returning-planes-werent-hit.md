# Did Wald really tell the military to armor where returning planes weren't hit?

Just show me the answer

TRUE

**True.** The reasoning in the famous story is Wald's. In his 1943 memoranda he estimated aircraft vulnerability from the damage on returning planes, while accounting for the planes that did not return.

Tell someone: Wald did reason that armor belongs where returning planes had the fewest bullet holes, since planes hit there did not come back.

## What's the version people tell?

The story is so neat that it is easy to doubt. A statistician looks at bombers riddled with holes and says to armor the places with no holes, because planes hit there never made it home. It sounds like a parable invented to teach a point.

## What's the source?

Cited in the book: Wald, "A Method of Estimating Plane Vulnerability Based on Damage of Survivors" (1943; Center for Naval Analyses reprint, 1980). Wallis, "The Statistical Research Group, 1942–1945: Rejoinder," *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 75 (1980). Elton, Gruber & Blake, "Survivorship Bias and Mutual Fund Performance," *Review of Financial Studies* 9(4) (1996). "Survivorship bias," Wikipedia.
